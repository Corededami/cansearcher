Why trials matter

Trials shape tomorrow's treatments, and help patients today

When a trial includes people from every background, doctors learn how a treatment works for everyone. When communities are missing, they miss out on new options, and new drugs reach the market without evidence for them.

  • Early access to new treatments not yet available elsewhere
  • The study treatment is usually provided at no cost
  • Close monitoring by a specialist care team
  • You can ask questions first and stop taking part at any time

Your privacy

CanSearcher does not ask for your name and does not save your answers. Trial listings come from ClinicalTrials.gov, the public registry run by the U.S. National Library of Medicine. Your answers are shared with our AI only to check which trials fit, and are not stored.

Questions

Common questions

What is a clinical trial?

A research study that tests a new way to prevent, find or treat cancer. Every new cancer treatment available today was first tested in a clinical trial.

Does it cost money to join?

The study treatment and study-only tests are usually free. Regular care may still be billed to your insurance. Ask the trial team what is covered, including travel and parking.

Is it safe? Will I be treated like a guinea pig?

Trials follow strict rules and are reviewed by independent boards that protect patients. Before you join, the team must explain the risks and benefits, and you can leave at any time for any reason.

Does CanSearcher decide if I qualify?

No. CanSearcher shows trials that may fit you. The trial team, together with your doctor, decides if a trial is right for you.

Who can use it?

Anyone: patients, family members, caregivers and doctors. It is free and works on any phone.
